# Open-Source Criminal Case Analysis (OSINT)

## About

This profile focuses on the independent analysis of real criminal cases using Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) and publicly available information.

It is a personal learning project intended to develop research, source evaluation, and analytical reasoning skills through practical case studies. Each study addresses a specific question and examines available information to identify relevant findings, assess possible relationships, and explore plausible explanations.

**I am not a specialist in OSINT or criminal investigation, nor am I a law enforcement professional.** This project represents independent study and analytical practice, not official investigative work or professional determinations.

## Objectives

- Develop practical OSINT skills through the analysis of real criminal cases.
- Identify, assess, and cross-reference information from publicly available sources.
- Examine relationships between events, individuals, organizations, and other relevant entities when supported by available information.
- Identify convergences, contradictions, information gaps, and alternative explanations.
- Distinguish source-reported information, corroborated findings, inferences, and hypotheses.
- Produce clear, structured, and traceable analytical outputs.
- Develop critical thinking and evidence-based reasoning when addressing specific questions.

## Published Work

Findings will be presented in the format best suited to the case and the question being examined. Publications may include:

- **Analytical Reports:** Structured presentations of relevant findings, source references, analysis, and conclusions.
- **Timelines:** Chronological representations of relevant events.
- **Relationship Diagrams:** Visual representations of supported connections between entities and events.
- **Tables and Matrices:** Structured comparisons and organization of relevant information.
- **Maps and Geographic Representations:** Spatial analysis when relevant to the case.
- **Other Analytical Visualizations:** Formats selected to communicate findings clearly and accurately.

Each publication will include the relevant sources, supporting information, reasoning, and limitations needed to understand and assess its conclusions. The focus is on the final analytical product, rather than a chronological account of every research action.

## Analytical Principles

- **Traceability:** Connect relevant claims to identifiable sources.
- **Source Evaluation:** Assess the origin, reliability, context, and consistency of information.
- **Corroboration:** Seek independent support for significant claims whenever possible.
- **Analytical Discipline:** Ensure that interpretations do not exceed what the available information supports.
- **Transparency:** Acknowledge uncertainty, missing information, and unresolved questions.
- **Alternative Explanations:** Consider plausible competing interpretations when relevant.
- **Responsible Publication:** Respect applicable laws, privacy, and ethical boundaries, and avoid unnecessary harm to people involved in a case.

## Scope and Limitations

This project examines existing criminal cases and related events through independent research using publicly available information. It does not conduct official investigations, exercise law enforcement authority, establish legal findings, or determine criminal responsibility.

The presence of information in a source does not automatically establish its accuracy. Likewise, the existence of corroborated facts does not necessarily validate every interpretation derived from them.

**All analytical conclusions are independent, provisional interpretations and must not be treated as official findings or definitive determinations of what happened.** An explanation may be plausible without being established. Alternative interpretations may remain possible, and conclusions may require revision when new information becomes available.

Allegations, suspicions, and apparent associations must not, by themselves, be treated as proof of criminal conduct or individual responsibility.

## Purpose

This project aims to develop practical competence in OSINT-based criminal case analysis by organizing and critically assessing publicly available information to address specific questions.

The value of each publication lies in the quality and traceability of its sources, the relevance of its supporting information, the clarity of its reasoning, and the appropriate treatment of uncertainty.

This is an ongoing learning process. The work is open to critical assessment and revision, and conclusions remain limited to what the available information reasonably supports.

**Research Question → Sources and Information → Analysis → Findings and Possible Explanations**

Independent research, critical analysis, and continuous learning.
